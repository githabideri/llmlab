# Qwen3.8-27B fallback inference on a dual-RTX-3060 (12GB) consumer desktop

**Date:** 2026-11-14 · **Status:** QUALIFIED_FOR_FALLBACK (all five qualification criteria measured; binding constraints named in §11) · **Companion:** [the 09-18 single-card study](2026-09-18-ista-3bit-27b-single-3060.md) ran this same model family in the same quantization family on one of these cards; this report is the dual-card qualification.

## Verdict

A consumer desktop (Intel i5-7400, 32GB DDR4, B250 chipset, two RTX 3060 12GB on a PHB layout with no PCIe peer-to-peer) runs Qwen3.8-27B — a 27B dense model with a hybrid sliding-window/SSM attention stack — at **measured 116,051 prompt + 1,024 decode tokens per concurrent session** (slot cap 131072; estimated ceiling ~126K, the 126-131K band was never probed; 256K pool, two sessions), with vision on a CPU projector, no observable freeze under a second consumer (worst-case gap 551 ms), a 12.7-second cold restart, and quantization fidelity that statistically ties a 4-bit reference on a 19-item objective battery. The serving engine is **llama.cpp (build b12383) with dual-card layer splitting**; vLLM cannot host this model on 12GB cards in either parallelization mode, for two different measured reasons.

## 2. Model and artifacts (frozen, hash-verified)

- **ISTA GSQ-RCO IQ3_S-mtp** (ISTA-DASLab / Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO), 12.1 GB, 3.0-3.4 bpw, with an integrated MTP head — the primary quantization.
- **Unsloth Dynamic V3.1 UD-IQ4_XS** (unsloth / Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF), 14.3 GB — the A/B control.
- Both with their **BF16 mmproj** projectors (0.93 GB each; vision runs on CPU, model on the GPUs).
- All three files were frozen by sha256 against their Hugging Face sources before any measurement; the serving build is **llama.cpp b12383** (released the same day, stock CUDA 13.2 build).

## 3. Engine verdict: why vLLM loses on this box (twice)

| Mode | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| vLLM 0.28.0, **TP2** | Measured 21-23 t/s decode at 16K with GPU SM utilization near zero and 3.6/4 CPU cores saturated | tensor-parallel all-reduce per step crosses the no-P2B PHB layout through system RAM; a 27B dense model does that 66 times per token |
| vLLM 0.28.0, **PP2** (TP1/PP2, fp8 KV, 64K) | Stopped at pre-registered condition SC1: the hybrid architecture *initializes* under pipeline parallel, but the W4A16 Marlin kernel repack hits a CUDA allocation failure on the second pipeline worker during model load, before any KV pool is sized, at both 0.90 and 0.85 utilization | the 4-bit Marlin repack peak exceeds 12 GB per card; a 27B 4-bit model does not fit a 12GB card the way the lighter MoE of March did |

The same 4-bit model on the same cards in llama.cpp's **layer split** (33/33 layers, weights resident on both cards, KV interleaved) runs at the numbers below. The lesson generalizes: for dense 27B-class models on no-P2P dual 12GB cards, the layer-split engine wins; vLLM's parallel modes both fail, and for different reasons. (The March 2026 PP3 recipe for the lighter MoE remains valid for that model class.)

## 5. Concurrency: the interesting result

Two properties of the workload matter: a *new* consumer arriving (its prefill monopolizes the GPUs), and *sustained co-decode* (both talking).

**Arrival.** A 16K/32K prefill arriving while a 114K-context session decodes stretches that session's inter-token gap to 441-551 ms (5-6x its 87 ms baseline) **for the duration of the arrival's own prefill** (6.8 / 26.5 / 55.7 s for 4/16/32K), then recovers to baseline within one token. **Across 16 runs, a single 5-second gap never occurred.**

**Sustained co-decode.** Both streams generating 1024 tokens at once: the newcomer runs at **10.3-10.6 t/s — essentially its full solo rate** — while the long session holds **7.8-8.6 t/s, a 24-31% reduction**, not the halving a symmetric GPU-split model predicts. Gap distributions stay tight (p95 ≤ 109.6 ms, max ≤ 115 ms). The plausible mechanism is the hybrid architecture: 16 of 66 layers are full attention, the rest carry fixed-size recurrent state, so a decoded token's cost is dominated by streaming the shared 12 GB of weights, which concurrent streams amortize. (Flagged as hypothesis; the rate data are the fact.)

Service statement: *a second consumer never freezes the long session; it costs it a crawl for the newcomer's prefill duration and roughly a quarter of its speed while both talk.*

## 6. Prefix cache

A repeated 114K prompt: first request 255 s TTFT (448 tok/s), second **1.33 s** (114,224 tokens served from cache). For multi-turn workloads the cold-prefill penalty is a once-per-session cost.

## 7. MTP (speculative decoding)

The b12383 wiring that works: `--spec-type draft-mtp` **alone** — the MTP draft context is created against the main model and shares its weights (extra cost: 1 GB f16 draft KV on card 1, recurrent buffers 0.3 → 1.2 GB, one more sequence). The naive `--spec-draft-model <same file>` loads a second full 27B copy and OOMs at boot.

Measured effect (ISTA, per-slot 256K/2, 2 reps per depth):

| depth | no MTP | MTP | acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16K | 18.4 t/s | 38.8 | 0.88-0.91 (2.9-3.7 drafts/step) |
| 64K | 14.0 | 27.5 | 0.72-0.79 |
| 120K | 11.0 | 20.4 | 0.64-0.67 |
| 126K | — | 20.6 (ceiling held) | 0.69 |

**Decode roughly doubles at every depth. Prefill gets ~40% slower** (120K: 345 s vs 244 s) because the draft context is built over the whole prompt. Net: for decode-heavy, multi-turn workloads MTP is the dominant config; for one-shot long-prefill workloads it costs more than it saves. The 126K-class probe (115,931 prompt) holds under MTP, but card-1 headroom at 256K/2 drops to ~40 MiB, so the MTP config is one step closer to the OOM edge than the no-MTP one (the no-MTP config passed the full reliability suite; MTP + 256K/2 + heavy concurrency was not stress-tested).

## 8. Quality battery (ISTA 3-bit vs Unsloth 4-bit)

19 matched items, identical harness/oracles, temperature 0, thinking off, both models on the identical dual-card config: 5 coding (exact-output oracles), 5 reasoning (exact answers, 1024-token budget), 3 tool-call (schema + argument match), 3 vision (build photos of the test machine, objective questions), 3 retrieval (planted facts in 16/64/120K word-soup context).

| | ISTA IQ3_S-mtp | Unsloth UD-IQ4_XS |
|---|---|---|
| coding | 5/5 | 4/5 (genuine computation error on longest-common-prefix) |
| reasoning | 4/5 (genuine 1024-token cap mid-derivation) | 5/5 |
| tool-call | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| vision | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| retrieval (16/64/120K) | 3/3 | 3/3 (120K at 218.3 s cold prefill) |
| **total** | **18/19** | **18/19** |

The two quantizations swap exactly one single-item failure and are otherwise indistinguishable on this battery. The selection criterion that decided: the 3-bit GSQ file is 2.2 GB smaller in VRAM with an integrated MTP head at equal battery performance. (A first battery run had shown 11/19; all eight misses were harness artifacts — truncation budgets and a tool-call parser that counted streamed JSON fragments — archived as superseded. Lesson: for streaming APIs, tool-call arguments must be assembled per index before parsing.)

## 10. Recommended configurations

**Primary (agent-style, multi-turn, concurrent consumers):** ISTA IQ3_S-mtp, `--ctx-size 262144 --parallel 2` (per-slot KV), q4_0 KV, `--jinja --load-mode none -t4 --flash-attn on`, **MTP on** (`--spec-type draft-mtp`). >=117K usable per session (estimated ~126K ceiling), 20 t/s decode at 120K depth, 38.8 t/s at 16K.

**Conservative (prefill-heavy one-shots, maximum headroom):** same, **MTP off** — 11-14 t/s decode at depth, ~440 MiB more headroom on card 1, and it is the configuration that passed the full reliability suite.

Both quantizations are worth keeping on disk (they tie on quality); the 3-bit is the better default. If the ~126K-class per-session ceiling is the hard line and MTP's 40 MiB card-1 headroom is uncomfortable, the measured fallback is 229K pool / 112K per slot (MTP headroom then has margin) — that is the one configuration step that trades the ceiling for safety.

**Co-residency constraint:** a 35B brain on the same cards cannot share them with this 27B server (both need the pair). The fallback server is cold by design; switch-over is the 12.7 s boot (measured) plus the brain's unload/reload (order of tens of seconds; not precisely timed in this campaign).

## 11. Binding constraints and open items

1. **Cold prefill at extreme depth is slow** (120K ≈ 4 minutes, i5-7400-bound; 4.5-5.5 minutes with MTP). This is the CPU, not the GPUs, and it does not improve with more VRAM. Multi-turn workloads pay it once (prefix cache: 1.3 s warm).
2. **Decode at depth is 11-20 t/s** — usable for agents, slow for interactive chat; the MTP switch is the lever.
3. **No P2P** caps anything that moves tensors between cards per step (this is what kills vLLM TP; layer split is unaffected).
4. **MTP + 256K/2 + heavy concurrency** was not stress-tested (40 MiB card-1 headroom); the no-MTP config passed full reliability.
5. Monitoring of the ad-hoc server is out of scope here; a production deployment would want it under a service manager with the box's existing exporters.
6. Wall power was not instrumented in this campaign (open gap); the companion 2026-08-30 report measured the same chassis class under 35B-class load and is the reference point for energy per token on these cards.
