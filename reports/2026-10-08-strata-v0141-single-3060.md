# 2026-10-08 Strata v0.1.41 on the single-3060 box: the 19th GiB of resident expert RAM breaks the deep-context plateau — 23 t/s at 90K, promoted to production

**Date:** 2026-10-08 (lane-0 window overnight 06–09; lane-1 windows 16:30–19:30 UTC and 20:12–22:05 UTC)
**Category:** Experiment / benchmark + production change
**Hardware:** backup / inference box — Intel i3-9100 (4C/4T, DDR4-2400 2-channel ≈ 38 GB/s), single RTX 3060 12 GB, 46.8 GB host RAM, **no swap**, NVMe, PCIe gen3 x16 (12.0–12.4 GB/s H2D probe). Container with a **42 GiB *hard* cgroup limit** (advisory stop line 40 GiB — never approached; max measured 38.9 GiB this lane, lifetime peak 39.8 GiB from the lane-0 era; zero `oom_kill`, zero allocation failures across all 12 measured cells and the host-level cgroup watcher).
**Model:** Qwen3.8-Flash-Next `GSQ-RCO-IQ2_XS` — the **same two 68 GB GGUF shards** as the [v0.1.39 report](2026-10-08-strata-v0139-single-3060.md) and the 10-05/10-07/10-08 OptLlama reports (SHA-256 recorded per run; the 51B PLE table is shard 2, the 39 GB expert set is split across the pack). **Engine:** [Strata](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata) **`v0.1.41`** (commit `fb58e0d`, src `62eb4907c`, CUDA 13.1) — the two local modifications are installer-only `setup.py` patches (skip re-staging the experts with `--low-ram mmap` + a local GGUF dir); the engine binary is otherwise the tag.

**What v0.1.41 changed vs v0.1.39** (the two deltas this campaign targets): **#1250** — a resident-RAM / cgroup-OOM mitigation (the engine now page-locks a *budget* of expert bytes into RAM instead of faulting the whole mmap under cgroup pressure — the exact failure mode that OOM-killed the box's earlier 40 GiB-limit run) — and a **stage-pin default flip**. Lane 0's job was to freeze a v0.1.41 baseline under *the old* regime (`STRATA_STAGE_PIN=0`, `STRATA_PREFILL_CPU_SHARE=0`) so that every v0.1.41 gain is measured against a v0.1.41 reproduction, not the historical v0.1.39 numbers.

**TL;DR**

- **Baseline (frozen, `BASE41`):** 98K max-context geometry — **16.71 t/s** decode at 16K (16 GiB resident budget), 16K prefill 73.7 s, true-cold 98K prefill **268.0 s**, warm 135.3 s; 32K geometry — **19.81 t/s**, prefill 38.8 s. In-band with v0.1.39 at both geometries (no regression from the two deltas; the stage-pin flip measures **−0.1%** on this 3060 — a no-op). Canary battery **byte-identical across four fresh engines** at both geometries.
- **Attribution (lane 0):** decode is **host-side** in every cell — per decode window the CPU expert compute (43.7–86.6 ms) + job queue (2.1–75.1 ms) dominate; the 3060 does <1.6% of the per-token time. The 4 cores, not the GPU, are the budget. PCIe gate **PASS** (gen3 x16 sustained under load; #1250 page-locks the full 16 GiB budget, 11,936 experts).
- **The lever (lane 1):** raising the resident budget **16 → 17 → 19 → 21 GiB** = 19.81 → 21.82 → **22.85** → 22.01 t/s at 16K/32K — the ladder peaks at **19 GiB (+15.3%)** and flattens. At the production 98K geometry the same move gives **23.02 vs 18.49 t/s at 90K (+24.5% within-window; +37.6% vs the frozen baseline, cross-window, labelled)**. Canary byte-identical at every rung.
- **Deep-cold prefill flips in the winner's favour (controlled `drop_caches` A/B, n=1 each):** the 19 GiB engine's frozen 98K prefill is **181.8 s** vs the 16 GiB engine's **240.7 s** — 24.5% *faster*, not slower (a mid-window uncontrolled re-run showed the opposite; the controlled A/B is the claim-bearing one). Mechanism is inferred (#1250 changes which pages are locked vs file-cached before the prefill), not established.
- **MTP is a mandatory positive component, not a tunable:** v0.1.41 **refuses `--spec 0`** for native (IQ) packs in *both* serve and generate modes ("needs --spec T (T ≥ 2)") — the old v0.1.39 off-MTP comparison is no longer expressible. The minimum expressible draft (spec 2) measures **15.03 t/s** in serve mode vs **19.81** for the setup's spec 4 → the spec-4 path is worth **≈ +32%** here. (In generate mode spec 2 ≈ spec 4, ~0.12 t/s, 16K/16 tokens — the generate path pays the verify cost regardless.)
- **Everything else chopped:** PCIe share flat across 0.2–0.75 (auto-resolved 0.34 sits on the plateau; 0.0 mildly negative), workers 2/4 both negative (3 is the shape of this box), the engine's own `--calibrate` is **net negative** (16.8 t/s headline; its pcie grid's "best 0.2" does not beat the dedicated 0.35 arm in the clean protocol) — same verdict as v0.1.39.
- **Promoted to production:** the lane-1 winner (98K / **19 GiB** / spec 4 / min-p 0.5 / KV int8 / mmap / auto expert-cache 3748 slots 5.04 GiB) is now the box's `qwen3.8-flash-next` serving config, on its own unit (port 8181, reachable from the agent host). **The box's 35B service is offline while Strata runs** — 42 GiB cgroup, they cannot coexist; that is the trade of the window, not a regression. It supersedes the llama.cpp expert-pool profile (10-09-29: ~18.8 / ~14 / ~8.5 t/s at 16K/32K/90K, ~95 t/s prefill) by **~2.7× at 90K decode** and roughly 2.5–5× on warm prefill.

**Method** — greedy, unique nonce per rep, 1 unscored warm-up + 3 scored 256-token reps (16K cells), every arm a fresh engine (serve) or fresh engine per rep (generate), canary battery per engine (three fixed questions whose answers are hash-compared against the lane-0 baseline; any mismatch invalidates the cell), 2–10 s sampling of cgroup (container + host level), NVMe sectors, and GPU VRAM/util/PCIe-replay, with host-level stop rules (sustained >40 GiB, any OOM). The 35B service was stopped for both windows and restored in between (live-verified); it was left down by design after the deployment. No wall-plug or GPU-power capture this lane (the samplers record VRAM/util/replay, not watts). Medians of 3; the published-claim n=5 confirmations are the remaining step of lane 2 (below), as is the r17 drift arbitration.

**Deep-cold A/B (98K frozen prompt, `drop_caches` before each fresh engine, n=1):** 19 GiB **181.8 s ttft / 196.9 s wall** vs 16 GiB **240.7 s / 254.0 s** → the 19th GiB's page-lock *evicts more cached expert-file pages before prefill*, and the net effect on the cold prefill is faster, not slower. The lexicographic "prefill retention" gate (winner must retain ≥90% of baseline prefill) passes with ~132–147% retention — the opposite of the feared #1250 cost.

**MTP** — see TL;DR. Practical consequence: `--spec 4 --spec-min-p 0.5` (the setup's defaults) are the only sensible MTP settings on this quant in v0.1.41; there is no "turn MTP off" to A/B, and the engine's own P2003 reference run (RTX 3060 + Core Ultra 7 265, IQ3_XXS) uses the same `--spec 4 --spec-min-p 0.5`.

**Production change (this box)** — new unit `strata-serve` (the box's inference container, restart-on-failure, `STRATA_STAGE_PIN=0 STRATA_PREFILL_CPU_SHARE=0`):

```
/opt/strata-v0141/engine/strata serve --config <cfg> --host 0.0.0.0 --port 8181
  --pack   <68 GB IQ2_XS pack>  --native <shard 1>  --ple-gguf <shard 2 (PLE)>
  --mtp    <RT draft>          --max-context 98304
  --kv int8 --mmap-experts
  --resident-budget-gib 19
  --spec 4 --spec-min-p 0.5
  --expert-cache auto          → 3748 slots / 5.04 GiB VRAM (auto)
  --prefill auto
```

Boot: model load + 19 GiB mlock + 98K geometry in ~70 s; first interactive request ~2.5 s overhead, then 15–17 t/s on short contexts and **~23 t/s at 90K** (the deep-context win is where it shows). Measured from the agent host over the private mesh; registered as an agent model card. **Window semantics:** while `strata-serve` is up, the box's 35B service is down (42 GiB cgroup — co-residency is impossible, same as the llama.cpp 125B's exclusive-GPU switch); stop `strata-serve` + start the 35B unit to give the box back.

**Remaining (lane 2, before the numbers are publish-grade):** n=5 on the two 19 GiB finalists (shallow + deep, arm order alternated per round) — the +15.3% / +24.5% are medians of 3; n=5 on the 17 GiB arm with per-rep tracking to arbitrate the drift anomaly; the cold A/B stays n=1 unless re-run. Then the campaign's verifier gate and publication.

**Conclusion**

The v0.1.39 read — "16 GB of cache-residency RAM is the ~1.6× lever; the >20 band is missed" — was directionally right and quantitatively stale: #1250 makes a *larger* resident budget safe under the 42 GiB cgroup (v0.1.39's pinned-path OOM history is precisely what it fixes), and on this 4-core box the budget ladder goes **19.81 → 22.85** at 16K and **18.49 → 23.02** at 90K before flattening at 21 GiB. The decode regime is still the 4 cores' expert compute, so the gain is the page-lock budget reducing fault traffic *around* that compute, not a change in the compute itself — and the same lever turns the cold-prefill regression fear upside-down (19 GiB cold-prefills *faster*). With MTP now a mandatory positive +32% component and the engine's own calibrate still net-negative on 4 cores, the whole v0.1.41 optimization surface on this box reduces to one number: **19 GiB**. That config is in production for the 125B model as of this report, at ~2.7× the deep-context decode of the llama.cpp profile it replaces — with the standing trade that the 35B is offline for the duration of the Strata window.

**Honest flags:** (1) the +24.5% deep and the cold A/B are n=3 and n=1 respectively; (2) the 17 GiB arm's downward ramp is unexplained (file-cache squeeze hypothesis, timing+memory evidence only) and is the one cell whose n=5 could move the ladder peak by one rung; (3) no energy capture this lane; (4) the v0.1.39 report's "not a production candidate" verdict was a throughput-vs-35B statement, not a verdict on the 125B's own serving — the deployment decision here is user-directed on exactly that model.
