# Serving Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 128K on a 6 GB Pascal laptop — 2026 llama.cpp, 580 LTSB, and the `cublasCreate_v2 = 3` crash

**Date:** 2026-09-25 → 2026-09-27 (three-day bring-up)
**Box:** HP OMEN 15 (2017) — i5-7300HQ (4 physical cores, **no Hyper-Threading on this unit**), GTX 1060 6 GB Max-Q (Pascal GP106, 1,280 cores, 60 W TGP), 24 GB DDR4 (asymmetric 16+8), headless NixOS, AC-only — full specs and the measured bandwidths in the [hardware profile](../docs/hardware/pascal-laptop.md)
**Model:** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (35B MoE, ~3B active, 256 experts, 10 of 40 layers full-attention) — `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-UD-IQ4_XS.gguf` (17 GB), text-only, **128K context, q8_0 K/V**
**Result:** in production as a hub-managed endpoint — **~11.8 t/s decode at 128K in 4.67 GB of VRAM**, ~167–186 t/s prompt processing, 38–50 W GPU

## Goal

Prove that the current (2026) llama.cpp generation can serve the current 35B-class MoE on the lab's Pascal hardware — the 580 LTSB driver (the last Pascal driver line, supported through mid-2028) and the CUDA 12.9 toolkit (the last one with `sm_61` — CUDA 13 dropped Pascal) — and put 128K context into a 6 GB card. The box is also the fleet's test bed for Pascal-only questions the 3060/3090 boxes can't answer (CPU-kernel dispatch, old-driver interaction, laptop PCIe/power topology).

## Setup

- **Engine:** llama.cpp 2026 (base commit of 2026-09-26), built from source: nixpkgs-24.05 gcc 13.2 + glibc 2.38 toolchain, `sm_61`, `GGML_CUDA_FA_ALL_QUANTS=ON`, `GGML_CUDA_GRAPHS=ON`, `GGML_CUDA_NO_VMM=ON`, CPU side `-march=native` (AVX2/FMA/F16C; no AVX-512/VNNI on Kaby Lake). Binaries are a stub + shared-library set with the interpreter and rpath pinned to persistent paths so they survive NixOS GC.
- **Driver:** 580.178.04 (LTSB). Pairing verified: driver 580 + toolkit 12.9.
- **Serving:** the 2026 `llama-server` in **router mode** (`--models-preset` INI carrying the ship config + per-model `load-on-startup`), which is what the llm-hub's `llama-router` card expects (model rows with id/status, per-model metrics, one-click load/unload).
- **Ship config:** `--load-mode none -ngl all -ncmoe 20 --moe-expert-cache-size 12 --moe-early-router --decode-overlap --backend-sampling --phase-aware-workspace -t 4 -c 128000 -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -b 2048 -ub 512`. Engine is the MoE-expert-cache fork (the 12-slot cache is the 128K enabler — see below).

## The `cublasCreate_v2 = 3` crash

The first weeks of this bring-up were blocked by a hard crash on the **first** GEMM of any GPU decode:

```
CUBLAS_CHECK(cublasCreate(&…))  →  cublasCreate_v2 returned 3 (CUBLAS_STATUS_ALLOC_FAILED)
```

triggered by *manual* `-ngl N` with the **default mmap** load mode. The forensics chain (strace → GDB → ltrace, all captured) established:

- The failure is inside `cublasCreate_v2` itself — no preceding CUDA call fails; the driver is healthy (every standalone probe: device query, 1×1 matmul through cuBLAS, `nvidia-smi` — passes).
- **Upstream main crashes identically to the fork** at the same commit era → not a fork bug, not a build bug.
- The trigger is the *combination* (manual layer placement + file-backed mmap + first-touch during allocation), not any single flag.
- The working combination — the one this node ships — is **`--load-mode none`** (whole model in pre-allocated host RAM, no mmap page faults during the first CUDA allocations) **plus `-ngl all -ncmoe N`** (placement via the CPU-MoE knob rather than a raw layer count) **plus `-t 4`**.

The 580 driver and the 2026 backend are otherwise stable on the card: with the correct load mode, months of serving produced no recurrence. (For what it's worth: in the 2026 base the cuBLAS handles are created lazily per device/stream, and the backend had no changes in the three weeks before this build — there is no recent regression to point at; the interaction is older than that window.)

## Thread count: decode is thread-insensitive, oversubscription hurts

This i5-7300HQ exposes **4 threads only** (SMT disabled in the machine's firmware — Kaby Lake HQ normally runs 4C/8T, this unit does not). Re-baselining the ship config (12-slot expert cache, 4K ctx, 256-token decode) across worker threads:

| Threads | Decode (t/s, ship config) |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 12.05 |
| 2 | 12.04 |
| 3 | 12.04 |
| 4 | 12.06 |
| 8 (2× oversubscription) | 9.7 (no-cache control run, −20%) |

**Decode throughput is flat from 1 to 4 worker threads** in the shipping cache configuration — additional CPU workers do not increase critical-path throughput. Only 2× oversubscription (8 threads on 4 physical cores) is harmful. Thread count is therefore **not a tuning lever** for the production path; the flat sweep matches the box's DRAM behaviour (STREAM shows ~19–20 GB/s with no thread scaling — see the bottleneck section), and the `-t 4` ship value is simply the fleet-standard one.

## 128K on 6 GB: the expert cache is the enabler

The context cliff on this card is a **VRAM** cliff, not an arithmetic one:

- 64K context, q8_0 K/V: ~1.0 GB of KV — fits, decode unchanged (12.4 t/s).
- 128K context: ~2.0 GB of KV. With the 17 GB IQ4_XS model's GPU-resident share, the stock engine runs out of VRAM and falls back to **host-RAM KV with per-token PCIe round trips → ~1 t/s**.

The shipped engine is the MoE-expert-cache fork: its **12-slot hot-expert cache** keeps the working set of routed experts in a compact GPU buffer (the cold source stays in host RAM, prefetched over the wire). That frees ~1 GB relative to the static-expert placement at equal CPU-residency — enough for the 128K q8_0 KV to fit:

| Config (fork, `-ncmoe 20`, 4K ctx) | Decode (t/s) | VRAM |
|---|---:|---:|
| no expert cache (control) | 12.42 | 4,053 MiB |
| **12-slot cache + 128K ctx (production)** | **11.78** | **4,671 MiB** |
| 24-slot cache | 13.47 | 3,877 MiB |

Two caveats that kept us honest: on this CPU/PCIe-bound card the cache's *decode* gain is small (+8% at 4K) — its value here is purely **freeing VRAM for the KV**; and the fork's headline number on a 5070 Ti (2.3×) does not transfer, because that gain comes from GPU-bandwidth-bound decode, which this 6 GB card is not. Grouped decode is fully active in production: `decode_grouped = 10,200`, `decode_legacy = 0`, all 40 expert groups, zero fallbacks.

## MTP: measured net loss on this card

`--spec-type draft-mtp` (the model's own MTP head, present in the IQ4_XS GGUF): 12.7 → **11.6 t/s** at 64K, **+850 MB VRAM**, draft acceptance 0.64 / mean 2.92 accepted. The draft head costs more than it saves when the *target* decode is CPU/PCIe-bound rather than GPU-compute-bound. Left off in the ship config; available as a flag.

## Bottleneck ranking (measured, not assumed)

A 5-minute 3,000-token decode on the production config, sampled 1 Hz (CPU clocks/temps, GPU state, memory):

- **CPU:** held **3.07–3.11 GHz on all four cores** the entire run (no throttle; 50–86 °C vs 100 °C critical) — not the limit.
- **GPU:** 99% util, **38–50 W against the 60 W TGP** (not power-throttled), ≤74 °C, ~1.2–1.7 GHz — not the limit.
- **PCIe:** Gen3 x8 negotiated under load (drops to Gen2/Gen1 when idle — normal power management), **~6.3 GB/s per direction sustained** — the expert prefetch fits comfortably; not the limit.
- **DRAM: consistent with the wall.** The asymmetric 16+8 DDR4 configuration measures **~19–20 GB/s sustained with no thread scaling** (a single-channel-equivalent profile; whether the BIOS interleaves the asymmetric DIMM set is not verifiable from the OS, so the mechanism stays a hypothesis and the measurement is the fact). Two independent measurements point the same way: the flat 1→4-thread decode sweep above, and the demand arithmetic — per active token the CPU-resident experts stream ~1.8–1.9 GB from RAM, i.e. **12 t/s × ~1.85 GB ≈ 22 GB/s, right at the measured ceiling**. The flat sweep alone would not prove DRAM-bound (any serialized memory path looks flat), but with the thread-insensitive STREAM curve it is the supported interpretation.
- The single highest-leverage hardware change on this machine is therefore **symmetric RAM** (2×16 → 32 GB dual-channel ≈ 2× the wall; 2×8 → 16 GB would also fit the 12 GB 2-bit model's CPU buffer — see the companion report).

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CPU-only (4C, full model in RAM) | ~23 | ~5–6 | ~25 | 0 |
| Production hybrid, 4K ctx | 186 (512) / 182 (2048) / 167 (8192) | **11.8 (128K) / 12.05 (4K, t1–t4 flat)** | 38–50 | 4.67 GB |
| ISTA 2-bit GSQ, all experts on CPU (companion report) | — | **4.87** (128-tok run) | n/a | **2.34 GB (floor)** |

Decode energy (measured, 300-token production decode @ 11.7 t/s): CPU package **13.3 W** (RAPL package domain; the core-domain counter on this platform is broken) + GPU **30–50 W** (1 Hz sampling over a 3,000-token run: 38.4 W avg, brief 67 W peaks) → ~55–70 W at the wall including platform (~10–15 W, not measured — no plug meter on a laptop).

## Conclusion

1. **The 2026 llama.cpp generation runs on Pascal** with the 580 LTSB driver and the last Pascal toolkit (CUDA 12.9) — the 35B-class MoE is servable on this hardware class, 128K included, with the current engine. The initial `cublasCreate_v2 = 3` failure was a config-specific interaction (manual `-ngl` + mmap), not a driver or fork defect; the fix is the load-mode/`-ncmoe` combination above.
2. **128K on a 6 GB card is a VRAM problem** solved by freeing ~1 GB with the 12-slot expert cache — at a decode cost the CPU/PCIe-bound card can absorb.
3. **Decode sits at the DRAM wall** (flat 1→4-thread sweep + thread-insensitive ~19–20 GB/s STREAM + the ~22 GB/s/token demand arithmetic all agree); CPU compute, GPU compute, GPU power, and PCIe have headroom. Symmetric RAM is the next step, and the numbers above are the baseline to compare it against.
4. The box is a hub-managed `llama-router` card (one-click load/unload, 202-accepted, ~5 min cold load on 4 cores) — the same control surface as the rest of the fleet, not a bespoke endpoint.
